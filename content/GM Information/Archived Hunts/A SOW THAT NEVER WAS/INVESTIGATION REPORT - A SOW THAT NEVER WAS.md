## Session Notes
---
**1. GENERAL OVERVIEW**

Quickly talk about the lore of CAIN.

Players play as Executioners, government drones with little to no free will who's objective is to hunt otherworldly entities known only as anomalies.

They have to investigate and answer the anomalies three trauma questions as well as locate it's palace and also erase the stain on reality.

The system is reactive, meaning players make all the decisions and I simply relay the consequences of those actions while also moving the game forward.

**2. DEBRIEF**

You find yourselves aboard a passenger train bound north for **Seattle, Washington**. The weather outside is cold, damp, and unwelcoming — a constant curtain of rain smears the window glass, turning the city lights beyond into long, bleeding ribbons of gold and red. It’s about **50°F**, and even inside the train there’s a lingering chill that clings to your coats and seeps through the seams of your gloves.

The rhythmic hum of the tracks beneath you is the only real comfort — that, and the soft chatter of a few passengers further down the car. The smell of brewed coffee mingles faintly with the scent of damp fabric and old metal.

Somewhere near Tacoma, the train jolts — a sudden, sharp bump that causes the lights to flicker for just a second. A man in a worn gray coat stumbles into your aisle, steadying himself on the headrest beside you. His eyes dart briefly toward your group — professional, assessing — before he gives a polite, mumbled apology.

“Tracks are rough this time of year,” he says with a half-smile, his breath misting faintly in the chill air. As he steps away, something slips from his gloved hand — a **manila envelope**, the kind you’ve seen too many times before. Bold black marker bleeds across the front in neat block letters: **C.A.I.N.**

The man doesn’t look back as he moves toward the next car.

You exchange a glance before opening the envelope. Inside are a few grainy photographs, a folded map of downtown Seattle, and a single printed page. The paper smells faintly of ozone and disinfectant — standard issue. The CAIN insignia is printed faintly across the top, half-obscured by a redacted header.

The text reads:
For the past twelve days, residents of The Marrow Residences, a luxury high-rise near downtown Seattle, have filed complaints about a **putrid, chemical stench** emanating from the penthouse suite. Residents have described the smell as "**unbearable**".

Standard authorities attempted entry twice but were forced to withdraw — all recorded personnel experienced violent nausea, disorientation, and mucosal bleeding within two minutes of exposure. No known chemical or biological agents match the symptoms.

City officials requested federal intervention. Case diverted through ███████████ and reassigned under CAIN jurisdiction.

THE MARROW:

The rain hasn’t stopped since you arrived.

The Marrow rises ahead of you — a spire of wet glass and black concrete, its upper floors veiled behind low clouds. From street level, the tower looks abandoned, though the faint flicker of warm light glows in a few lower windows.

As you make your way inside, the lobby is silent except for the hum of fluorescent lights and the rhythmic drip of water collecting in a cracked bucket by the door. A concierge desk sits unmanned, the phone still off the hook. The air smells faintly metallic, as if the rain itself were rusting the walls.

An old elevator sits at the end of the hall, the panel glowing weakly. When you press the button, the doors creak open with a metallic groan. The floor directory has been stripped down to its steel backing — only one crooked label remains:  
**“FLOORS 42–50 — RESTRICTED.”**

A laminated notice taped beside the panel reads:  
_“Maintenance closure until further notice. Management access only.”_

There’s a **card reader** bolted beneath the call panel, you'll most likely need a **keycard** to use this elevator.

Somewhere deep in the walls, you hear the low hum of machinery — or maybe the faint vibration of something alive.

Whatever’s on those upper floors, it’s locked tight… for now.

The stairwell access is **sealed**, but the front desk’s terminal and maintenance office still have power. Someone else might have the card that you need to access the elevator.

The Husband - Viktor Mulder

You visit the hotel Viktor is staying at, it's only a few blocks away from The Marrow, it's quiet when he answers the door.

**Viktor Mulder**, early-30s, immaculate suit, and the faint smell of cologne that can’t quite cover the scent of stale liquor. He looks you over with a politeness that feels rehearsed. Behind him, the penthouse is spotless — cleaner than any space occupied by grief should be.

“Oh… right, the investigators,” he says, stepping aside. “They told me someone might come by.”

As you make your way in, you notice the smell of women's perfume lingering in the air.

Ask him about his wife, and the penthouse?

He speaks little about his wife, "I made most of my fortune in real-estate. I bought this property as a gift to my wife when we married a few years ago. Last week I was on a *business trip* and I planned to be back in time to spend time with my wife. She's currently pregnant with our son. I returned home the second I was informed about the penthouse. I haven't seen my wife since. You have to find her, I don't want anything happening to my son."

His eyes don’t wander toward her framed photographs — or the half-packed suitcase by the couch.

On the table lies an unopened envelope from **The Marrow’s building management**, marked _“Urgent.”_ He hasn’t touched it.

The conversation stalls until his phone buzzes. He stiffens slightly, moving to silence it. For a second, the screen lights up enough for you to see the name: **“Robin (Office).”**

That was my “*secretary*.” he says.

There’s no grief in his face — only irritation that you’re still standing here. When you ask if you can follow up later, he waves absently, already looking past you.

He’s hiding something — and whoever _Robin_ is, she might know what.

A little digging into his office building downtown, or perhaps a casual visit with the secretary herself, might turn up more than he expects.

---

Fire Station #211

The rain turns to mist as you near **Fire Station #211**, a squat brick building two blocks from The Marrow. Its floodlights cut faint cones through the fog, and the red engines inside gleam wet under flickering fluorescents.

Captain **Lena Ruiz** meets you at the door — tired eyes, coffee mug, the faint smell of smoke and disinfectant. She doesn’t ask for credentials; one look at you and she just nods.

Ask her about The Marrow?

“You’re here about The Marrow,” she says quietly. “Yeah. We were there. Twice.”

She hands you all masks and you swiftly put them on. She leads you into the equipment bay. The air is damp and metallic. A row of hazmat suits hangs from the ceiling — their fabric blackened, slouched, and **rotting**. The visors have collapsed inward, the filters melted into soft tar.

“That happened in less than an hour,” Ruiz mutters. “We tried cleaning them. Then sealing them. Now they just… decay.”

One of her crewmen points to the corner where a locker sits half-open. Inside, flashlights and batteries have corroded to green sludge.

“We can’t even keep the lights on near them,” he says. “Everything short-circuits.”

Ruiz folds her arms, looking back at you. “Whatever’s in that building doesn’t like electricity. Or air. Or… people.”

She offers to let you inspect the gear — under supervision — but warns you to keep your masks on.

“If you find out what the hell that smell is,” she says, “you let me know. We’ve been breathing it for a week.”

The firehouse feels safe enough for now… but something about the blackened gear hums faintly, like static on the edge of hearing.
