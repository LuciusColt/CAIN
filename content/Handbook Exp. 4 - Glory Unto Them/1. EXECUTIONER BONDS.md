## EXECUTIONER BONDS 

**DEEP IN MY HEART**

CAIN unabashedly touts it as part of their centuries-spanning dogma: **Executioners are not human**. So it was written, long ago, in the Doctrine of Worms, a near-medieval parchment text said to now adorn the central meeting room of AUTHORITY, set in a glass case in the wall.

However, while the letters of that mummified goatskin scroll writhe in the minds of all who serve CAIN, they do not change this fact:

**Executioners long for connection to one another**. Each of them possesses that exceptionally human weakness. That font of pathos, of love and of loss.

It is precisely for this reason that many Executioners serve CAIN at all. The chance to be with one another, to be seen, held, needed, no matter how briefly and at what cost of great self-destruction. To their battered hearts, even the disdainful treatment of CAIN is acknowledgement.

There is a saying:

“*Hungry dogs will eat crap.*”

**Executioner Bonds** are a way for executioners to improve their abilities through personal connection, while at the same time risking greater suffering should something happen to their closely held peers.

When an executioner takes an action that would **grant another executioner Agenda XP** and that executioner is in the scene with them, these two executioners can create a shared three-slash **bond tag** for the mission. Slash it each time:

- One of these executioners sets up the other. 
- Both of these executioners are part of a teamwork action. 
- One of these executioners sacrifices something meaningful for the other.

When the bond tag is filled, these executioners gain a **bond**, based around beliefs they each have about one another. Here’s an example:

>RANK: **B**  
>**Sun**, *according to Fan*  
>"Sun is such a drama queen that I cannot believe he’s trying for clout with AUTHORITY. He’s a menace, hah!”

>RANK: **B**  
>**Fan**, *according to Sun*  
>"I like how earnest Fan is and how little she cares about what she says. That girl is wickedly funny.”

**BOND EFFECTS**  

The benefits of having Bonds are as follows:

- If an executioner has at least two Bonds, their cap for **pathos** increases by 1. If a character has one for each team member, or at least one S-level Bond, it increases by 1 again. 
- Bonds can be **progressed** from level B to A and then to S by the same process as gaining a Bond. 
- Bonds can be **broken** to create pathos at the cost of strain. Level B Bonds grant 2 strain and 1 pathos when broken, to be divided amongst the executioners to whom the Bond belonged. 
- Higher level bonds create more extreme effects when broken: **1d3 strain and 3 pathos** for A Bonds, **1d3+1 strain and 4 pathos** for S Bonds. 
- A single Bond can only be progressed once per mission, and not in the same mission that the Bond was gained in. 
- Progressing a Bond grants the involved executioners 1 XP each.

**PROGRESSED BONDS**

A and S Bonds should be interpreted as the connection between characters growing **more intense**, not necessarily more positive or affectionate. For instance, this is a way a bond could progress:

>RANK: **B**  
>**Sun**, *according to Qin Gyi*  
>“I often say I wish Sun would stop spotlighting me and hyping me up, but I secretly enjoy it a little.”

>RANK: **A**  
>**Sun**, *according to Qin Gyi*  
>“I feel like it’s impossible to make friends on my own, so without my brother Sun, I’d be miserable.”

>RANK: **S**  
>**Sun**, *according to Qin Gyi*  
>“I’ll let the whole world die a miserable death before I let anyone take Sun away from me.”

However, this is also a possible progression of a bond:

>RANK: **B**  
>**Sayaka**, *according to Valentine*  
>“Sayaka seems like the kind of person who loves to see people suffer. Heh. She’s like a little centipede.”

>RANK: **A**  
>**Sayaka**, *according to Valentine*  
>“I just know Sayaka has a rotten personality under that aloof veneer. I’ll expose it to the world.”

>RANK: **S**  
>**Sayaka**, *according to Valentine*  
>“Sayaka is turning everyone against me. I can’t lead a normal life until I completely humiliate her.”

**BREAKING BONDS**

The act of breaking a Bond does not have to mean that the characters betray each other or go from friends to enemies.

It means that these characters now have to rebuild their relationship from the ground up. Whatever it was before has ended.

The way players agree to distribute strain and pathos between their executioners when they break their bond should reflect the fiction of their confrontation.

As a shorthand, characters who feel *guilty* or *vengeful* when breaking a bond should get the most strain, and ones who feel *hurt* or *vulnerable* should get the most pathos. These are intentionally not mutually exclusive.

To complicate the lives of executioners, an Admin may (within reason) declare certain actions impossible to take without breaking specific Bonds in doing so, like treason, murder, or leaving someone behind.

Bonds can be broken at any time by characters’ narrative actions. If multiple Bonds break in one scene, **only the highest level Bond** (choose one if tied) generates strain and pathos.

Breaking a Bond grants the involved executioners 1 XP each.
