### **COMMUNAL DINING FACILITY (“THE GARDEN TABLE”)**
---
###### ***🜃 “As Above, So Below.”***
---
**Document Reference**: W12–L2–CATERING–008
**Location**: Level 2, adjoining the Mirror Garden Biosphere
**Operational Status**: Continuous; serves three scheduled meals per day and one optional night ration

##### **I. OVERVIEW**
The Garden Table functions as the cafeteria for all residential-level personnel and held individuals at Watchsite-12.
Meals are structured and are uninterrupted. The environment is designed to mimic a university commons or hospital dining hall, softening the psychological impact of confinement.
The ceiling above the cafeteria is a continuation of the Mirror Garden’s dome, allowing the Antarctic light (filtered through meters of ice) to refract down, diffused in ever-shifting blues.
Artificial daylight cycles maintain a comforting rhythm; at night, overhead fixtures simulate aurora movement.
Sound is intentionally dampened, and conversations occur in hushed tones. Most meals pass in near-silence — the only noise the muted clatter of trays and the hum of ventilation.

##### **II. LAYOUT AND DESIGN**
* **Capacity**: 150 persons
* **Seating**: Rounded polymer tables for four; no sharp edges or corners.
* **Serving Line**: Automated tray dispensers with staff oversight.
* **Décor**:  Minimal — soft grey flooring, living plants from the Mirror Garden integrated into corners and centerpieces.
* **Atmosphere**: Warm, dry, faint scent of mint and sterilized metal.
Along the east wall stands a panoramic viewport that opens directly to the Garden. Residents often sit facing it, watching hydroponic trees sway under artificial wind generators.

##### **III. MENU AND MEAL STRUCTURE**
Meals at Watchsite-12 are standardized across residents and low-level staff, carefully calibrated for nutritional balance and emotional regulation.
Every meal includes a mild serotonergic supplement, diluted in water or tea, to subtly maintain mood stability.
**Meal Schedule**:

| Time (UTC) | Meal                  | Description                                                                                                     |
| ---------- | --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **07:30**  | Breakfast             | Oat porridge or eggs (powdered), tea, a slice of nutrient bread, fruit substitute (apple gel).                  |
| **13:00**  | Lunch                 | Soup (root vegetable or lentil), hydroponic greens, protein cube (poultry, soy, or algae-based), water.         |
| **18:30**  | Dinner                | Warm stew or pasta analog, steamed greens, bread roll, tea, and optional dessert ration (pudding or fruit gel). |
| **21:30**  | Optional Night Ration | Herbal tea and biscuit for residents experiencing insomnia or night simulations. Must be logged.                |

Each tray bears a resident’s XID stamped on the corner.
Food texture is soft and uniform — intentionally non-stimulating to reduce agitation responses in high-resonance individuals.

##### **IV. PSYCHOLOGICAL AND SOCIAL DYNAMICS**
The cafeteria doubles as an informal data collection point. Seating arrangements, conversation clusters, and appetite levels are monitored by on-site Behavioral Analysts for signs of instability or emerging hierarchies.
Executioner candidates typically sit in predictable patterns — solitary figures at the edge, pairs of quiet conversation, or groups clustered under the dome’s light.
Dr. Hart encourages “voluntary seating shifts” every two weeks to promote emotional flexibility.
> [[Lucian]] is noted to sit facing the glass, tray half-finished, sketchbook sometimes open beside his food. He rarely finishes meals but is compliant.

Occasionally, Dr. Hart joins the residents for tea — a rare gesture seen as symbolic of equality. Her presence calms the room but also quiets it further.

##### **V. SPECIAL EVENTS AND RITUALS**
Despite the isolation, the Garden Table observes several unofficial traditions:
* **“Blue Day”** — On the anniversary of the facility’s founding, meals are tinted faint cobalt using natural dye from spirulina cultures.
* **The Empty Tray**: Once a month, an unclaimed tray is placed at an empty table for “those who didn’t return from simulation.” Residents leave small folded napkins or paper cranes on it.
* **Green Hour**: Occasionally, the lights dim and simulated sunlight floods the dome — allowing residents to dine under “warm daylight.” Music is played softly (usually piano).
